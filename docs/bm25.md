# How do I rank passages by BM25 in the local RAG?

The local RAG today ranks the 2779 passages of a collection by the cosine of
their TF-IDF vectors, optionally followed by a cross-encoder rerank. BM25 is a
different ranking from the same term counts, with two extra knobs: a saturation
`k1` that flattens the effect of repeating a term, and a normalisation `b` that
compensates for long documents. On the 12 questions whose source paper is
known, BM25 ranks the expected paper first for 9 of them, against 7 for both
TF-IDF systems; on 3 questions the three systems differ, and at 12 questions
the difference is not significant.

## 1. What the score means, in words

For a query `q` and a document `i`, BM25 sums over the query terms a term
score that is large when the term is rare in the collection and frequent in
`i`, and that saturates: the tenth occurrence of a term adds much less than
the first. The length normalisation `b` compensates for documents that are
longer than average, so a long document does not win just because it contains
every term many times.

## 2. The formula, every symbol

The term score is the one of Silajev (2026, arXiv:2609.14016, page 1), with
the `+1` correction in the IDF:

    BM25(t, i) = d_i(t) * (k1 + 1) / (d_i(t) + k1 * (1 - b + b * |d_i| / avgdl))
                 * ln((N - DF(t) + 0.5) / (DF(t) + 0.5) + 1)

- `d_i(t)` is the count of the term `t` in the document `i`;
- `|d_i|` is the length of `i`, and `avgdl` the average length of the collection;
- `N` is the number of documents, and `DF(t)` the number of documents that contain `t`;
- `k1` is in `[0, inf)`: the saturation;
- `b` is in `[0, 1]`: the length normalisation.

The query score is the one of the same paper, page 4:

    Score(q, i) = sum over t of ( q(t) / |q| ) * ( BM25(t, i) / (k1 + 1) )

where `q(t)` is the count of `t` in the query and `|q|` the query length. The
division by `k1 + 1` is the one of page 4, not a choice of this implementation.
The `+1` inside the logarithm is the one of page 1, again not a choice.

## 3. A small case, worked out by hand

Three short documents and the query `a b`: `["a a b", "a c", "b b b"]`, with
`k1 = 1.2` and `b = 0.75`. The document frequencies are `DF(a) = 2`,
`DF(b) = 2`, `DF(c) = 1`. The IDFs are

    IDF(a) = IDF(b) = ln((3 - 2 + 0.5) / (2 + 0.5) + 1) = 0.470004
    IDF(c) = ln((3 - 1 + 0.5) / (1 + 0.5) + 1) = 0.980829

The lengths are 3, 2 and 3, and `avgdl = 8 / 3`. The three scores for the query
`a b` are `0.243510`, `0.118988`, `0.163480`. They are written as constants in
`tests/test_bm25_index.py` and are matched by the code to `1e-12`. The code is
not checked against a second copy of its own formula; the test computes them
once from the formula of page 1 and page 4, with a small Python script, and the
script is not the library.

## 4. The index, batch and online

The index is built once, either in batch or online.

```python
from bm25_index import BM25Index

idx = BM25Index(k1=1.2, b=0.75).fit(["a a b", "a c", "b b b"])
print(idx.n_docs_)
print(idx.top_k("a b", k=2))
```

`fit` builds the state from a list of documents and `learn_one` builds the
same state one document at a time; the test pins that the two end in the same
state. The class is an index, not an estimator: `query` returns a numpy array
of scores, `top_k` returns the best `(index, score)` pairs with ties broken by
the lower index, and `state_dict` / `load_state_dict` write JSON, never
`pickle`. A query with no known term gives zeros, never `nan`; `k1 < 0`, `b`
outside `[0, 1]` and a `stop_words` string raise a `ValueError`.

## 5. The tests, with a reference that does not contain the formula

The tests live in `tests/test_bm25_index.py`. The hand case of section 3 is
written as numbers in the file, and is the reference that does not contain the
formula. `test_fit_and_learn_one_same_state` and
`test_fit_and_learn_one_same_scores` pin the batch and online forms against
each other. `test_idf_positive_when_term_in_every_document` (the term is in
every document, the `+1` keeps the IDF positive) and
`test_b_zero_independent_of_length` cover the two knobs.
`test_state_dict_roundtrip_identical_scores` covers the JSON state.

## 6. The benchmark on the 12 quantum RAG questions

`benchmarks/bm25_vs_tfidf.py` ranks the 2779 passages of the collection with
three systems on the 12 questions whose source paper is known: the
`BM25Index` of section 4, the Dense-Armor `TextVectorizer` + `SparseCosineIndex`
on the same tokenizer, and the scikit-learn
`TfidfVectorizer(analyzer=stem_tok.analyze)` that the repository uses today.
The script prints the table, the per-question ranks, the raw score ranges,
and a paired Wilcoxon p-value and an A12 effect size on the reciprocal ranks
for each pair of systems.

| System | Build (s) | Query median (ms) | Rank 1 | Rank 3 | Rank 5 | RR median | RR IQR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bm25 | 0.31 | 4.756 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 1.000 | [0.773, 1.000] |
| dense_tfidf | 0.95 | 0.391 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 1.000 | [0.160, 1.000] |
| sklearn_tfidf | 1.40 | 6.927 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 1.000 | [0.173, 1.000] |

Pairwise on the reciprocal ranks, paired Wilcoxon, A12 with a bootstrap 95%
interval over 2000 resamples:

| Pair | p | A12 | A12 CI95 | Wins A | Wins B | Ties |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bm25 vs dense_tfidf | 0.1250 | 0.566 | [0.490, 0.674] | 4 | 1 | 7 |
| bm25 vs sklearn_tfidf | 0.6250 | 0.556 | [0.469, 0.667] | 2 | 2 | 8 |
| dense_tfidf vs sklearn_tfidf | 0.5000 | 0.483 | [0.375, 0.587] | 1 | 4 | 7 |

The three systems differ on 3 questions out of 12:

| Question | Expected | bm25 | dense_tfidf | sklearn_tfidf |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | juan2016_field_aware | 1 | 3 | 3 |
| 9 | juan2016_field_aware | 1 | 5 | 1 |
| 12 | jacobsen2003 | 1 | 1 | 5 |

On these 12 questions, BM25 ranks the expected paper first for 9 questions,
against 7 for the two TF-IDF systems. The paired Wilcoxon p-values (0.1250,
0.6250, 0.5000) do not reject the null hypothesis that the three systems are
the same on these 12 questions, and the A12 effect sizes (0.566, 0.556, 0.483)
with 95% bootstrap intervals that contain 0.5 do not establish a difference.
**Twelve questions are too few for a claim that BM25 beats TF-IDF.** The table
is a measurement on a small set, not a ranking of the three systems in general.

## Details

### References

- Silajev, I. (2026). *TF-IDF and BM25 Are Exact KL Divergences.*
  arXiv:2609.14016. Page 1: the BM25 term score and the `+1` correction in the
  IDF. Page 4: the `Score(q, i)` that sums `q(t)/|q|` times
  `BM25(t, i)/(k1 + 1)`, and the original IDF without the `+1` (discussed on
  the same page, not used in this implementation).
- Robertson, S., Zaragoza, H. (2009). *The Probabilistic Relevance Framework:
  BM25 and Beyond.* Foundations and Trends in Information Retrieval 3(4):
  333-428. doi:10.1561/1500000019. Cited by Silajev (2026), bibliographic only.
- Robertson, S. E., Walker, S., Jones, S., Hancock-Beaulieu, M. M., Gatford, M.
  (1994). *Okapi at TREC-3.* In Proceedings of TREC-3, NIST Special Publication
  500-225, 109-126. Cited by Silajev (2026), bibliographic only.

### Choices stated explicitly

- `k1 = 1.2` and `b = 0.75`: the paper gives `k1` in `[0, inf)` and `b` in
  `[0, 1]` and no default for either (page 1). The values 1.2 and 0.75 are
  the common choice in the BM25 literature and are "my choice", not a value
  of the paper.
- The tokenizer is the Dense-Armor `Tokenizer` with unigrams only. The same
  stop words as `stem_tok.analyze` (scikit-learn `ENGLISH_STOP_WORDS`) are
  passed to both, so the term inventories of the three systems differ only
  by the stemmer and the bigrams.
- The `+1` IDF of page 1 is used; the original IDF of page 4 is not.
- The three systems differ on 3 questions out of 12, as the table in section
  6 shows; the pairwise statistics are on those 12 reciprocal ranks and are
  reported with the full p-value, A12 and bootstrap interval.
- `bm25_index.py` does not import `scikit-learn` or `nltk`; the benchmark
  imports them at run time for the comparison.

### Measured cost

On the 2779 chunks of the collection `ml_streaming_algoritmi` and the 12
questions, on this host:

- `BM25Index(k1=1.2, b=0.75)` builds in 0.31 s and answers a query in 4.756 ms
  (median);
- `TextVectorizer` + `SparseCosineIndex` builds in 0.95 s and answers a query
  in 0.391 ms (median);
- `TfidfVectorizer(analyzer=stem_tok.analyze)` builds in 1.40 s and answers a
  query in 6.927 ms (median).

The full benchmark runs in 5.42 s.

### Raw score ranges

Before ranking, the scores of the three systems are not on the same scale:

- `bm25` (unnormalised): min 0.0, max 2.3424, mean 0.0617;
- `dense_tfidf` (cosine, L2 normalised): min 0.0, max 0.5352, mean 0.0066;
- `sklearn_tfidf` (cosine, L2 normalised): min 0.0, max 0.5098, mean 0.0091.

The two cosine systems are comparable in magnitude; BM25 is not, and a direct
comparison of a BM25 score against a cosine would be meaningless. The
benchmark compares the ranks, not the raw scores.

### The differences between the three systems

| System | Score | Length normalisation | IDF | Tokenizer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bm25 | `Score(q, i)` of page 4 | `b * abs(d) / avgdl` with `b = 0.75` | `ln((N - DF + 0.5) / (DF + 0.5) + 1)` (page 1) | Dense-Armor `Tokenizer`, unigrams, scikit-learn stop words, no stemmer |
| dense_tfidf | cosine of L2-normalised TF-IDF vectors | L2 norm of the vector | `ln(N / DF)` without `+1` (the `TextVectorizer` of the current release) | same `Tokenizer`, unigrams, scikit-learn stop words, no stemmer |
| sklearn_tfidf | cosine of L2-normalised TF-IDF vectors | L2 norm of the vector | scikit-learn `TfidfVectorizer` default (smooth) | `stem_tok.analyze`: Snowball stemmer, scikit-learn stop words, bigrams |

The three differences are the tokenizer (unigrams against bigrams with a
stemmer), the IDF (`+1` against no `+1` against scikit-learn's smooth default),
and the score (a saturating sum against a cosine).
